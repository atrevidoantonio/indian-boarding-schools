Indian Boarding Schools in the United States
OverviewSchoolsTimelineSources

Between 1828 and 1970, at least 3,104 Native children died at Indian boarding schools in the United States.

The federal government and the churches ran more than 500 schools to remove children from their nations. This map records 521 of them, who operated each one, and the years they were open.

Death count: The Washington Post, 2024 · Schools: NABS and NCTR inventory

Schools

Map: 521 schools with coordinates in the National Native American Boarding School Healing Coalition (NABS) and National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation inventory, retrieved November 2026. Operator categories follow the inventory's operator field and notes; schools without a recorded church or federal operator are shown as not recorded. Open schools are counted through 1969, the end of the period covered by the Interior Department's investigation. Reservation boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau, American Indian, Alaska Native and Native Hawaiian Areas, 2023. School narratives quoted in inventory records are written by the U.S. Department of the Interior. School-level death counts: The Washington Post, data-boarding-schools (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), matched to inventory records by name and state; 3,093 of the Post’s 3,104 deaths fall at schools on this map, and the remaining 11 are at two schools the inventory does not map. The Post counted deaths only at the 417 federally funded schools on the Interior Department’s list. Circle size scales with the square root of the Post’s count. Death counts are minimums. The headline figure is The Washington Post’s 2024 count of 3,104; the Interior Department’s 973 covers federal schools only.
